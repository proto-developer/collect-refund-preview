How Unclaimed Property Works in New York

The New York State Comptroller’s Office of Unclaimed Funds (OUF) is the agency responsible for safeguarding billions of dollars in lost or forgotten money. New York’s program operates under the Abandoned Property Law, which dates back to 1943 and is one of the oldest such programs in the country.

When a financial account or asset has had no owner-initiated activity for the dormancy period, generally three years, the holder is legally required to turn it over to New York State. From that point, the state holds the funds indefinitely on behalf of the rightful owner. There is no deadline to claim and no statute of limitations.

What Types of Property Are Held by New York

  • Bank and brokerage accounts
  • Stock dividends and securities (including shares from mergers, spin-offs, and corporate actions)
  • Life insurance proceeds and annuities
  • Court-deposited funds (settlements, escrow, judgment funds)
  • Estate and trust distributions (especially common in NYC)
  • Pension and 401(k) distributions
  • Utility and rental security deposits (short 1-year dormancy applies)

How to Search the New York Unclaimed Funds Database

The official search portal is at ouf.osc.ny.gov. Practical tips:

  • search every spelling of your name (initials, maiden names, hyphenated combinations)
  • every New York address you have held (dorms, sublets, corporate office addresses)
  • relatives who lived or worked in New York
  • former employers as the holder name
  • don’t stop at MissingMoney.com — the state’s own search is the authoritative source

How the New York Claim Process Works

New York has invested heavily in modernizing its claim process. Many smaller claims can now be initiated and verified entirely online. Larger or more complex claims still follow a documentation-heavy path.

A typical claim packet requires:

  • completed claim form (generated by the OUF online portal)
  • government-issued photo ID
  • proof of address connection to the listed property
  • for inherited property: death certificate, will or letters testamentary, proof of heirship
  • for securities: documentation of original ownership and account history
  • for business claims: corporate documentation, EIN, signing authority
  • for larger claims: a notarized claim affidavit

Simple cash claims often resolve in 30–90 days. Securities, estate, and business claims commonly run 4–9 months.

What Makes New York Different

  • Massive securities exposure. Because of Wall Street, New York holds an outsized share of stock-related unclaimed property. Many of the country’s largest unclaimed securities cases route through New York.
  • Faster dormancy on some categories. Utility deposits and unclaimed wages have a 1-year dormancy period, among the shortest in the country.
  • Daily disbursement. New York returns roughly $1 million per business day to claimants, but still has tens of billions remaining.
  • Online-first claim experience for small claims. Cash claims under a defined threshold can often be verified and paid almost entirely online.

Frequently Asked Questions: New York Unclaimed Property

How much unclaimed property does New York hold?

The New York State Comptroller’s Office of Unclaimed Funds holds over $17 billion in lost or forgotten money, including bank accounts, securities, insurance proceeds, court funds, and estate distributions.

Where do I search New York unclaimed funds?

The official search is available at ouf.osc.ny.gov. The database is free, public, and run directly by the New York State Comptroller’s Office.

How long does a New York unclaimed property claim take?

Simple cash claims often resolve in 30 to 90 days. Securities, estate, and business claims typically take 4 to 9 months and may require multiple rounds of supplemental documentation.

Does New York pay interest on unclaimed funds?

New York pays interest on certain interest-bearing accounts that earned interest before being escheated to the state, but does not pay additional interest on most non-interest-bearing property.

Can I claim unclaimed property from a deceased family member in New York?

Yes. Heirs can file claims with appropriate documentation, including a death certificate, letters testamentary or letters of administration, and proof of heirship. Estate claims are one of the most common categories handled by CollectRefund in New York.

Is there a deadline to claim unclaimed property in New York?

No. New York has no deadline. The state holds unclaimed property indefinitely until the rightful owner or their legal heirs file a valid claim.
