How Unclaimed Property Works in Colorado

Colorado’s unclaimed property program is administered by Colorado State Treasurer’s Office, Great Colorado Payback, which acts as the legal custodian of dormant accounts, uncashed checks, securities, and other lost financial assets reported by holders doing business in the state. Colorado operates under a state version of the Uniform Unclaimed Property Act, which sets the rules for when financial institutions, employers, insurers, and other holders must turn over inactive accounts.

When a financial account, paycheck, refund, or insurance benefit goes inactive for the legally defined dormancy period (generally 5 years (and as little as 1 year for unclaimed wages)), the holder is required to escheat the asset to the state. Colorado then holds the property in trust indefinitely until the rightful owner or heir files a valid claim. There is no deadline to claim and no statute of limitations.

Colorado does not keep the money. It serves as a custodian, returning funds to anyone who can document their ownership.

What Types of Property Are Held by Colorado

  • Bank accounts and uncashed checks
  • Stocks, dividends, and mutual funds
  • Insurance proceeds
  • Unclaimed wages
  • Utility deposits
  • Mineral and royalty proceeds

Beyond these headline categories, Colorado also holds smaller volumes of court-deposited funds, mineral and royalty proceeds, trust distributions, and contents of unclaimed safe deposit boxes.

How to Search the Colorado Unclaimed Property Database

The official Colorado unclaimed property search is at greatcoloradopayback.com, accessible from the Colorado State Treasurer’s Office, Great Colorado Payback website. To maximize your odds of finding a match, work through this checklist:

  • Search every name variation (maiden names, married names, hyphenated combinations, common misspellings, nicknames)
  • Search every Colorado address you have ever held, including college addresses, military housing, and prior corporate addresses
  • Search former Colorado employers as the holder name, particularly large employers in Denver, Colorado Springs, Aurora, Boulder, since payroll-related claims are one of the most common categories
  • Search relatives who lived or worked in Colorado, especially older relatives whose pensions, annuities, or insurance policies may have escheated
  • Don’t rely solely on MissingMoney.com — Colorado participates in the national database, but the Colorado state portal is the authoritative source and updates first

How the Colorado Claim Process Works

Colorado has invested in modernizing its claim portal in recent years. Many low-dollar cash claims can now be initiated and verified online; larger or more complex claims follow a documentation-heavy path.

A typical Colorado claim packet requires:

  • A completed claim form generated through the Colorado portal
  • Government-issued photo identification
  • Proof of association with the listed property (utility bills, bank statements, pay stubs, or prior tax returns showing the address of record)
  • For inherited property: death certificate, letters testamentary or letters of administration, will, and proof of heirship
  • For business claims: corporate records, EIN documentation, and proof of signing authority
  • For securities: documentation of original ownership and any corporate action history
  • For higher-value claims: a notarized claim affidavit, often required above a defined dollar threshold

Simple cash claims often resolve in 90–150 days. Securities, estate, business, and multi-state claims commonly run 6–12 months and may require multiple rounds of supplemental documentation.

When to Consider Professional Help

Filing a small, straightforward cash claim with Colorado on your own is usually practical. Working with a recovery firm makes sense when the claim is more involved:

  • Estate or inheritance claims with multiple heirs, probate complications, or out-of-state beneficiaries
  • Securities and stock claims with corporate action history since escheatment
  • Business and institutional claims for entities that have been merged, dissolved, or restructured
  • Multi-state claims
  • Higher-value claims (typically $5,000+)
  • Previously denied claims

How much unclaimed property does Colorado hold?

The State Treasurer’s office for Colorado holds over $700 million in unclaimed property, over a million properties. Roughly 1 in 7 to 1 in 10 Americans has unclaimed property in their name across the U.S. system, and Colorado is no exception.

Where do I search Colorado unclaimed property?

The official Colorado unclaimed property search is at greatcoloradopayback.com, accessible from the Colorado State Treasurer’s Office, Great Colorado Payback website at greatcoloradopayback.com. The database is free, public, and updated regularly.

What is Colorado’s dormancy period?

Most property types in Colorado have a 5-year dormancy period before escheatment to the state. Unclaimed wages have a shorter 1-year dormancy.

How long does a Colorado unclaimed property claim take?

Simple cash claims often resolve in 90 to 150 days. Securities, estate, business, and multi-state claims commonly take 6 to 12 months and may require multiple rounds of supplemental documentation.

Is there a deadline to claim unclaimed property in Colorado?

No. Colorado holds unclaimed property indefinitely. There is no deadline to claim and no statute of limitations on filing, the rightful owner or their legal heirs can come forward at any time.
