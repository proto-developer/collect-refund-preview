How Unclaimed Property Works in California

California’s unclaimed property program is administered by the State Controller’s Office (SCO) under the Unclaimed Property Law (Code of Civil Procedure §§ 1500–1582). When a financial account, paycheck, refund, or insurance benefit goes inactive for the legally defined period, typically three years, the institution holding it is required to escheat the asset to the state. The state then holds it indefinitely until the rightful owner or heir files a claim.

California does not keep the money. It holds it in trust. There is no deadline to claim, and there is no penalty for waiting, but California does not pay interest on most accounts after escheatment, so claiming sooner is usually better.

What Types of Property Are Held by California

  • Bank accounts and uncashed checks: dormant savings, checking, and CD accounts, plus payroll, vendor, and refund checks that were never cashed
  • Stocks, bonds, and mutual fund shares: investment accounts that fell out of contact, including dividends and dividend reinvestments
  • Life insurance benefits: policies where the beneficiary was never notified or could not be located
  • Safe deposit box contents: physical items from boxes that went unpaid for 3+ years
  • Unclaimed wages: final paychecks, bonuses, expense reimbursements, and severance from former employers
  • Utility and rental security deposits: refundable deposits never collected after a move
  • Court settlements and class-action proceeds: judgment funds that were never disbursed
  • Estate distributions: inheritances where heirs were never located

How to Search the California Unclaimed Property Database

The California State Controller’s Office maintains a public, searchable database at ucpi.sco.ca.gov. Search tips that materially improve your results:

  • Search every name variation: maiden name, married name, nicknames, hyphenated forms, and common misspellings
  • Search every California address you have ever held, property is often listed by the address of record at the time the account went dormant
  • Search deceased family members who lived or worked in California, unclaimed inheritance is one of the largest single categories
  • Search business names: including DBAs, predecessor entities, and former corporate names if you ever owned a California-registered business
  • Don’t rely on the national MissingMoney.com search alone: California participates, but the state’s own database is the authoritative source

How the California Claim Process Works

Filing a claim with California involves several steps and supporting documents. For straightforward, low-dollar claims, doing it yourself is usually practical. For higher-value claims, multi-state matches, securities, or estate-related claims, the process becomes more complex.

The standard claim packet typically requires:

  • A completed claim form (downloadable from the SCO website)
  • Government-issued photo identification
  • Proof of association with the property (bank statements, pay stubs, utility bills, prior tax returns)
  • For inherited property: death certificate, probate documents, letters testamentary, and proof of heirship
  • For business claims: corporate records, EIN documentation, and authority-to-claim documentation
  • For securities: documentation showing original ownership of the shares

California typically processes simple claims within 60–180 days. Complex, securities, or estate claims often take 6–12 months and may require multiple rounds of supplemental documentation.

What Makes California Different

  • No interest after escheatment. Most accounts stop earning interest the moment they’re transferred to the state. This is a significant difference from a handful of other states.
  • Securities are sold to cash within a defined window. California liquidates escheated stocks and securities, typically within 18 months. If you claim afterward, you receive the cash value at the time of sale, not the current market value of the shares.
  • Aggressive dormancy schedule. A 3-year dormancy period means California captures inactive assets faster than 5-year states like Georgia or Hawaii.
  • Annual unclaimed property reporting season. Holders (banks, employers, insurers) report to California every year before November 1, meaning the state’s database refreshes substantially each fall.

What to Know About California’s Unclaimed Property Program

  1. California holds the largest unclaimed property fund in the United States, more than any other state.
  2. Roughly 1 in every 7 Californians has unclaimed property in their name.
  3. California uses a 3-year dormancy period for most property, which is shorter than many states, meaning property is escheated to the state more quickly.
  4. The State Controller’s Office returns hundreds of millions of dollars to Californians every year, but a far larger amount remains unclaimed.

Frequently Asked Questions: California Unclaimed Property

How much unclaimed property does California hold?

California holds over $12 billion in unclaimed property and is the largest unclaimed property fund in the United States. Tens of millions of properties are listed in the state database, and roughly one in seven Californians has unclaimed property in their name.

Where do I search for unclaimed property in California?

The official California State Controller’s Office unclaimed property search is available at ucpi.sco.ca.gov. The database is free, public, and updated regularly.

How long does it take to get unclaimed property back from California?

Simple cash claims usually take 60 to 180 days. Securities, estate, business, and multi-state claims commonly take 6 to 12 months and may require additional documentation rounds.

Does California pay interest on unclaimed property?

California does not pay interest on most unclaimed property after the funds have been escheated to the state. Claiming sooner rather than later is generally better.

What happens to unclaimed stocks and securities in California?

California typically liquidates escheated securities within a defined window after escheatment. If you claim after the sale, you receive the cash value at the time of sale, not the current market price of the shares.

Is there a deadline to claim unclaimed property in California?

No. There is no deadline. The state holds unclaimed property indefinitely until the rightful owner or their legal heirs come forward and file a valid claim.
