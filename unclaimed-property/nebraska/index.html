How Unclaimed Property Works in Nebraska

Nebraska’s unclaimed property program is administered by Nebraska State Treasurer, Unclaimed Property Division, which acts as the legal custodian of dormant accounts, uncashed checks, securities, and other lost financial assets reported by holders doing business in the state. Nebraska operates under a state version of the Uniform Unclaimed Property Act.

When a financial account, paycheck, refund, or insurance benefit goes inactive for the legally defined dormancy period (generally 5 years, and as little as 1 year for unclaimed wages), the holder is required to escheat the asset to the state. Nebraska then holds the property in trust indefinitely until the rightful owner or heir files a valid claim. There is no deadline to claim and no statute of limitations.

Nebraska does not keep the money. It serves as a custodian, returning funds to anyone who can document their ownership.

What Types of Property Are Held by Nebraska

  • Bank accounts and uncashed checks
  • Insurance proceeds and annuities
  • Stocks and dividends
  • Pension distributions
  • Unclaimed wages
  • Agricultural and grain settlement payments

Beyond these headline categories, Nebraska also holds smaller volumes of court-deposited funds, mineral and royalty proceeds, trust distributions, and contents of unclaimed safe deposit boxes.

How to Search the Nebraska Unclaimed Property Database

The official Nebraska unclaimed property search is at treasurer.nebraska.gov, accessible from the Nebraska State Treasurer, Unclaimed Property Division website. Checklist:

  • every name variation
  • every Nebraska address (college, military housing, prior corporate addresses)
  • former Nebraska employers as the holder name (large employers in Omaha, Lincoln, Bellevue, Grand Island)
  • relatives who lived or worked in Nebraska
  • don’t rely solely on MissingMoney.com — the Nebraska state portal is the authoritative source and updates first

How the Nebraska Claim Process Works

Nebraska has invested in modernizing its claim portal in recent years. Many low-dollar cash claims can now be initiated and verified online; larger or more complex claims follow a documentation-heavy path.

A typical Nebraska claim packet requires:

  • completed claim form generated through the Nebraska portal
  • government-issued photo ID
  • proof of association with the listed property
  • for inherited property: death certificate, letters testamentary or letters of administration, will, proof of heirship
  • for business claims: corporate records, EIN documentation, proof of signing authority
  • for securities: documentation of original ownership and corporate action history
  • for higher-value claims: a notarized claim affidavit

Simple cash claims often resolve in 90–150 days. Securities, estate, business, and multi-state claims commonly run 6–12 months.

  • securities and stock claims with corporate action history
  • business and institutional claims for merged, dissolved, or restructured entities

Frequently Asked Questions: Nebraska Unclaimed Property

How much unclaimed property does Nebraska hold?

The State Treasurer’s office for Nebraska holds over $250 million in unclaimed property, hundreds of thousands of properties. Roughly 1 in 7 to 1 in 10 Americans has unclaimed property in their name across the U.S. system, and Nebraska is no exception.

Where do I search Nebraska unclaimed property?

The official Nebraska unclaimed property search is at treasurer.nebraska.gov, accessible from the Nebraska State Treasurer, Unclaimed Property Division website at treasurer.nebraska.gov/up. The database is free, public, and updated regularly.

What is Nebraska’s dormancy period?

Most property types in Nebraska have a 5-year dormancy period before escheatment to the state. Unclaimed wages have a shorter 1-year dormancy.

How long does a Nebraska unclaimed property claim take?

Simple cash claims often resolve in 90 to 150 days. Securities, estate, business, and multi-state claims commonly take 6 to 12 months and may require multiple rounds of supplemental documentation.

Is there a deadline to claim unclaimed property in Nebraska?

No. Nebraska holds unclaimed property indefinitely. There is no deadline to claim and no statute of limitations on filing, the rightful owner or their legal heirs can come forward at any time.
