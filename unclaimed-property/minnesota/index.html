Minnesota Unclaimed Property: How to Find & Claim Your Money

Minnesota’s Department of Commerce holds over $1 billion in unclaimed property, money belonging to current and former Minnesota residents waiting to be claimed. With the Twin Cities serving as the corporate headquarters for several Fortune 500 companies, Minnesota’s unclaimed property fund is heavily weighted toward payroll, pension, and securities claims.

How Unclaimed Property Works in Minnesota

Minnesota’s unclaimed property program is administered by the Department of Commerce, Unclaimed Property Division. The program operates under Minnesota Statutes Chapter 345, the Uniform Unclaimed Property Act.

When a financial account, paycheck, refund, or insurance benefit has had no owner-initiated activity for the dormancy period, generally three years, the holder is legally required to escheat the property to the state. Minnesota then holds it in trust indefinitely. There is no deadline to claim and no statute of limitations.

What Types of Property Are Held by Minnesota

  • Bank accounts and uncashed checks
  • Insurance proceeds
  • Stocks and dividends (from Minnesota-based corporations — Target, UnitedHealth, 3M, U.S. Bancorp, General Mills, Best Buy, Ecolab — and out-of-state firms)
  • Utility and rental deposits
  • Unclaimed wages (1-year dormancy, the shortest category)
  • Estate and trust distributions
  • Healthcare-related payments (overpayments and refunds, especially around the Mayo Clinic ecosystem)

How to Search the Minnesota Unclaimed Property Database

The official Minnesota search is at mn.unclaimedproperty.com, accessible from the Department of Commerce site at mn.gov/commerce. Search strategy:

  • every name variation (incl. common Scandinavian-name spelling variations)
  • every Minnesota address
  • former Minnesota employers as the holder name (Twin Cities Fortune 500 employers and Mayo Clinic-related entities)
  • relatives who lived or worked in Minnesota
  • both MissingMoney.com and the Minnesota state portal — the state portal is authoritative

How the Minnesota Claim Process Works

Minnesota has modernized its claim process significantly. Many low-dollar cash claims can be initiated online, while larger or more complex claims follow a documentation-heavy path.

A typical Minnesota claim packet requires:

  • completed claim form (generated by the Minnesota portal)
  • government-issued photo ID
  • proof of association with the property
  • for inherited property: death certificate, letters testamentary, will, proof of heirship
  • for business claims: corporate records, EIN, signing authority
  • for securities: documentation of original ownership and corporate action history
  • for higher-value claims: a notarized claim affidavit

Simple cash claims often resolve in 60–120 days. Securities, estate, and business claims commonly run 6–12 months.

When to Consider Professional Help

Working with a recovery firm makes sense when:

  • the claim is estate-related with multiple heirs or out-of-state beneficiaries
  • involves securities with corporate action history since escheatment
  • involves wages, RSUs, or stock from a major Twin Cities employer that has merged, restructured, or spun off divisions
  • is multi-state (common for executives, contractors, and traveling healthcare workers)
  • is higher-value (typically $5,000+)

What Makes Minnesota Different

  • Department of Commerce, not Treasury. Correspondence and forms come from the Department of Commerce, not the State Treasurer.
  • 3-year dormancy. Property reaches Minnesota more quickly than 5-year dormancy states like Georgia or Wisconsin.
  • Twin Cities corporate concentration. A disproportionate share originates from large Twin Cities headquarters: Target, UnitedHealth, 3M, U.S. Bancorp, General Mills, Best Buy, Ecolab, and others.
  • Healthcare sector volume. Minnesota’s large healthcare industry, anchored by the Mayo Clinic, generates a steady stream of unclaimed wages, vendor refunds, and patient-overpayment claims.

Most Common Property Types in Minnesota

  • Bank accounts and uncashed checks
  • Insurance proceeds
  • Stocks and dividends
  • Utility and rental security deposits
  • Unclaimed wages
  • Estate and trust distributions

Cities With the Most Unclaimed Property in Minnesota

  • Minneapolis Largest concentration of corporate, financial, and pension claims
  • Saint Paul Government, healthcare, and insurance claims
  • Rochester Healthcare-sector claims, particularly Mayo Clinic-related employment
  • Duluth Industrial, port, and pension claims
  • Bloomington Corporate and retail-sector claims
  • Brooklyn Park Manufacturing and corporate claims

What to Know About Minnesota’s Unclaimed Property Program

  1. Minnesota’s unclaimed property program is administered by the Department of Commerce, distinct from many states that use the Treasurer’s office.
  2. Minnesota uses a 3-year dormancy period for most property types, which is shorter than many neighboring states (Wisconsin uses 5 years).
  3. The Twin Cities area generates a disproportionate share of unclaimed property because of its concentration of Fortune 500 headquarters: Target, UnitedHealth, 3M, U.S. Bancorp, General Mills, Best Buy, and Ecolab.
  4. Minnesota returns tens of millions of dollars per year to rightful owners while continuing to accumulate new escheated property each reporting cycle.

Frequently Asked Questions: Minnesota Unclaimed Property

How much unclaimed property does Minnesota hold?

The Minnesota Department of Commerce holds over $1 billion in unclaimed property, with millions of individual properties listed in the state database.

Where do I search Minnesota unclaimed property?

The official Minnesota search portal is mn.unclaimedproperty.com, accessible from the Department of Commerce site at mn.gov/commerce.

Why does the Department of Commerce handle unclaimed property in Minnesota?

Minnesota is one of a small number of states that houses its unclaimed property program under the Department of Commerce rather than the State Treasurer’s office. This is a structural choice and does not affect your right to claim.

How long does a Minnesota unclaimed property claim take?

Simple cash claims often resolve in 60 to 120 days. Securities, estate, and business claims commonly take 6 to 12 months.

What is Minnesota’s dormancy period?

Most property in Minnesota has a 3-year dormancy period before escheatment. Unclaimed wages have a much shorter 1-year dormancy.

Is there a deadline to claim unclaimed property in Minnesota?

No. Minnesota holds unclaimed property indefinitely. There is no deadline to claim and no statute of limitations.
