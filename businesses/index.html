What it takes to claim.

States look closely at corporate history before releasing funds. Straightforward active-entity claims often resolve in a few months, while dissolved or acquired entities take longer due to required succession documentation.

Authority to act

States verify that the person filing can act for the entity. What that takes depends on status:

  • Active entities: an officer, member, or authorized representative files, with corporate records proving the role.
  • Dissolved entities: states want documentation of who is entitled to the property after wind-down, such as final owners or their successors.
  • Acquired & merged: the transaction records that connect the reporting name to today’s claiming entity.

Burden of proof

Requirements vary by state, claim, and entity status. We identify what each claim needs and do the assembly work:

  • Entity-history reconstruction: formation records, name changes, mergers, and dissolution filings that connect the reported name to your organization.
  • Ownership documentation: operating agreements, stock records, and succession documents that establish who is entitled to the funds.
  • Address & identity evidence: records tying the reported address or account to your entity’s operating history.

Business questions, answered

Common questions about business recovery and monitoring.

Can CollectRefund recover property owed to my business?

Yes. States hold unclaimed property reported under business names, vendor checks, refunds, deposits, insurance proceeds, and more. CollectRefund searches records tied to your entity names, past addresses, and predecessor entities, then guides eligible claims through the state process.

What does business recovery cost?

There is no upfront cost to search or begin the process. If CollectRefund successfully recovers funds, a service fee applies based on the claim and applicable state requirements. The exact fee is disclosed before recovery is authorized.

Our company was dissolved years ago. Can we still claim its property?

Often, yes. States hold property for dissolved entities, and claims can typically be made by former owners, officers, or successors with the right documentation. Requirements vary by state, claim, and entity status: CollectRefund identifies what is required and helps assemble the proof.

Who is allowed to claim on behalf of a business?

States require proof that the person filing has authority to act for the entity, for example an officer, member, authorized representative, or successor in interest. CollectRefund confirms who can sign for your organization and what authority documentation the state expects before anything is filed.

We operate in many states. Do we have to search each one separately?

No. CollectRefund searches across jurisdictions for records tied to your entity names, former names, subsidiaries, and address history. Managed end-to-end recovery currently operates in All 50 states, and CollectRefund provides search results and official filing guidance elsewhere.
